Space Woven from Threads: the Story of Loop Quantum Gravity

The idea that space itself is made of tiny pieces, like a fabric woven from threads, and that there is a smallest possible area.

Chapter 1. Is space smooth?

Look at a sheet of silk and it seems perfectly smooth. Put it under a microscope and you see threads, woven over and under. Einstein’s gravity treats space like silk seen from far away: smooth, bendable, with no smallest piece.

But quantum physics says energy comes in lumps, and light comes in particles. Why should space be different?

Chapter 2. A new way to write gravity

In 1986 Abhay Ashtekar found a new way to write Einstein’s equations. It made them look much more like the equations for the other forces. Two years later Carlo Rovelli and Lee Smolin used it to build a quantum version of space itself.

In their picture, space is a network of threads, called a spin network (an idea first imagined by Roger Penrose in 1971). Each thread carries a tiny amount of area. Where threads meet, there is a tiny amount of volume. Space is not a stage on which things happen. It is the network.

Chapter 3. The smallest area

The most striking result came in 1995. Rovelli and Smolin calculated that area cannot take any value. It comes in steps, like the rungs of a ladder. There is a smallest possible area, a few times the square of the Planck length. That is about a hundred billion billion times smaller than a proton, squared.

So space has a grain, like sand on a beach. From far away the beach looks smooth. Up close it is grains.

Chapter 4. Black holes and the beginning

Loop quantum gravity can count the grains on the surface of a black hole, and so count its hidden states. To get Bekenstein and Hawking’s answer, though, it has to set one number by hand, the Immirzi parameter (about 0.24).

Applied to the beginning of the universe, the theory suggests something surprising. There was no single point of infinite density. The universe may have bounced, shrinking from an earlier phase and rebounding (Martin Bojowald; Ashtekar, Tomasz Pawlowski and Parampreet Singh).

Chapter 5. What it has not done

Loop quantum gravity is a theory of space and gravity only. It does not explain the particles, their masses or the forces. It also has not yet fully shown that, viewed from far away, its network of threads becomes the smooth space of Einstein’s gravity. No measurement has yet seen the grain of space.

The theory at a glance

Main idea Space is a quantum network; area and volume come in smallest steps
New ingredients none beyond Einstein’s gravity and quantum rules; one number set by hand (Immirzi)
Got right black-hole entropy, once the Immirzi number is set
Not yet the particles; a confirmed prediction; the smooth large-scale limit in full

How it differs from TRPG

  • The shared picture. Both see space as something real with a smallest grain. TRPG’s “grain of space” is close in spirit.
  • Particles. Loop quantum gravity covers gravity only. TRPG tries to cover the particles too.
  • A test between them. In November 2026 TRPG tested whether loop quantum gravity’s smallest area could set TRPG’s grain. It could not: the natural reading puts the grain 2.27 times below the Planck energy, which needs a strong-force strength of 0.1153 against a measured 0.1180. That combination failed. The two pictures may both be right about grains, but not with the same grain.
