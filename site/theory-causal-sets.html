Order Plus Number: the Story of Causal Sets

The idea that space and time are made of separate events, like grains of sand, and that all you need to know is which came before which.

Chapter 1. Counting the grains

How big is a beach? You could measure it with a tape. Or you could count the grains of sand. If you know roughly how much room one grain takes, the count tells you the size.

In 1987 Luca Bombelli, Joohan Lee, David Meyer and Rafael Sorkin suggested that space-time is like that. It is made of separate points, or events, each about the size of the Planck scale. To rebuild all of geometry you need only two things:

  1. Order: for any two events, which one could have caused the other, if either.
  2. Number: how many events there are in a region, which tells you how big it is.

Sorkin summed it up as “Order + Number = Geometry.”

Chapter 2. Why order is enough

It sounds too simple, but mathematicians (David Malament in 1977, and Stephen Hawking with colleagues) had already shown that if you know which events can affect which, you know almost all of the shape of space-time. The only thing missing is a sense of size, and counting supplies it.

The events are sprinkled at random, like raindrops on a pavement, so that no direction is special. That keeps Einstein’s rule that motion is relative.

Chapter 3. A prediction about the dark

In the late 1980s, years before anyone knew the expansion of the universe was speeding up, Sorkin reasoned as follows. If the number of events is like a count of raindrops, it cannot be exact. It must wobble by about the square root of the number. That wobble should show up as a tiny leftover energy in empty space, a cosmological constant, of roughly the size

1 ÷ √(number of events in the universe’s past).

When dark energy was discovered in 1998, its size matched that rough estimate. (Maqbool Ahmed, Scott Dodelson, Patrick Greene and Sorkin worked it out in more detail in 2004, calling it “everpresent Λ”.)

Chapter 4. What is missing

Causal sets are a theory of space and time only. They do not yet produce particles or forces. The dark-energy estimate gets the size right but not the exact value: it carries a free number of order one and predicts that dark energy wanders up and down over time.

The theory at a glance

Main idea Space-time is a set of discrete events with a before-and-after order; counting gives size
New ingredients none beyond the grain itself
Got right the rough size of dark energy, reasoned before its 1998 discovery
Not yet particles and forces; an exact number

How it differs from TRPG

  • The shared picture. “Counting the points gives the volume” sits close to TRPG’s full glass: space is completely filled, with no gaps.
  • A test between them. In November 2026 TRPG tested Sorkin’s dark-energy estimate. It got the size within a factor of 0.72. But it could not replace TRPG’s measured dark-energy number, because the estimate carries a free factor and a random sign. It also says dark energy wanders at random, while in TRPG it is the bag’s kept amount: one measured number that need not be constant, with no law yet for how it changes.
  • What would decide. If DESI’s hints that dark energy changes over time firm up, the pattern of the change decides: a random wander favours the causal-set picture.
