Equations explained

Every equation used by the framework, with a statement of what it does, the role it plays here, and who first wrote it down.

The framework is summarised by a single action. Most of its terms are the established physics of general relativity and the Standard Model; the framework contributes the containment field, the scale field and the relational form of the lepton masses. Part I explains the action term by term. Part II explains the further equations used to compare the framework with measurement. The complete derivations are on the Mathematics page.

Part I. The action, term by term

1. Gravity

(c⁴/16πG) R
What it does
The Einstein–Hilbert term. R is the curvature of spacetime; varying it gives Einstein's field equations, which say that energy and momentum curve spacetime and that curvature guides motion.
Role in the framework
The term is not added by hand. It is the large-scale consequence of entanglement in the medium: an entanglement entropy proportional to boundary area, together with the Clausius relation at local horizons, implies Einstein's equations.
Origin
Einstein (1915); Hilbert (1915); the thermodynamic derivation is Jacobson (1995).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Einstein's field equations.

2. Cosmic baseline

− ρΛ
What it does
A constant energy density that fills all of space. It drives the present accelerating expansion.
Role in the framework
Read as the baseline resistance of the medium at the largest scale. Its value, about 3.3 × 10⁻⁴² MeV/fm³, corresponds to an energy scale of 2.24 meV, which lies within the range of the predicted neutrino masses. On the main line its size is set by the grain and the horizon of space together (√(grain × horizon) = 3.5 meV); its value is measured, and no law for how it changes is derived.
Origin
Einstein (1917); measured value from Planck (2018).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Why the vacuum's energy does not weigh.

3. Containment field

− ½(∂P)² − (λ/4) v⁴ (P² − 1)²
What it does
A scalar field P with two states: P = 1 outside hadrons and P = 0 inside. The energy difference between the two states is a pressure that confines quarks.
Role in the framework
This is the containment principle written as a field. The depth of the potential equals the pressure B = 60 (57–64) MeV/fm³, set by the proton and Δ masses; its curvature is set by the lightest glueball (1.71–1.73 GeV). Together they give λ ≈ 1160–1210 and v ≈ 35 MeV, with a wall thickness of about 0.11 fm. Nothing is adjusted.
Origin
Friedberg and Lee (1977), soliton bag model; glueball mass from Morningstar and Peardon (1999).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The containment field (the bubble wall).

4. Scale field

− (∂σ)², m = m₀ e2σ
What it does
A field that records the scale at which a mass is viewed. Shifting σ multiplies all masses by the same factor.
Role in the framework
This is the relational-scale principle. It makes the distance between two masses S = ½ ln(m₁/m₂), the only measure unchanged by a common rescaling. The geometry of this scale space is not fixed but follows the superposition of waves, and averages to flat.
Origin
Running with scale: Gell-Mann and Low (1954), Wilson (1971).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Relational distance and the scale field.

5. Forces

− ¼ FμνFμν
What it does
The kinetic terms of the gauge fields: electromagnetism, the weak force and the strong force, with symmetry U(1) × SU(2) × SU(3).
Role in the framework
The measured carrier counts (eight with two neutral, four with two neutral) allow only SU(3) and SU(2) × U(1) (Killing–Cartan classification). The medium's starting principles restrict its inner algebra to the division algebras (Hurwitz), of which 𝕆 gives SU(3) with three colours: the strong force is the one group derived from those principles. On the main relationship-matrix line the weak force, hypercharge and one-handed matter follow as well, with one stated reading (the perspective). Only the strengths remain: they meet if the strong coupling is 0.1178, which awaits measurement. That forces are local symmetries follows from relativity and quantum mechanics (Weyl, Yang and Mills). With three colours, anomaly cancellation fixes the quark charges at +2/3 and −1/3.
Origin
Yang and Mills (1954); Glashow, Weinberg and Salam; division-algebra programme: Hurwitz (1898), Dixon (1994), Furey (2016), Chamseddine and Connes.

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: One spot, one facing: the forces.

6. Matter

ψ̄ iγμDμ ψ
What it does
The Dirac term for the quarks and leptons of all three generations. Dμ couples matter to the forces.
Role in the framework
Taken unchanged.
Origin
Dirac (1928).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The master equation (the action).

7. Containment coupling

− gP P ψ̄qψq
What it does
Couples quarks to the containment field. Where P = 1, quarks are heavy and cannot exist freely; where P = 0, inside a hadron, they move almost freely.
Role in the framework
This is how containment acts on quarks: the push of quark motion balances the pull of the field.
Origin
Friedberg and Lee (1977).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The containment field (the bubble wall).

8. Lepton masses

− (√2/vH) ℓ̄ Sℓ² ℓ H, Sℓ = M₀ · circ(1, eiδ/√2, e−iδ/√2)
What it does
The Higgs coupling that gives the charged leptons their masses, written through a 3 × 3 circulant matrix of square-root masses.
Role in the framework
This is the relational principle applied to three generations: equal self-terms, equal links of 1/√2 and a twist δ = 0.222222, a measured number equal to 2/9 to about one part in a million. One scale M₀ gives the electron, muon and tau masses to 0.003%. The same matrix applied to neutrinos is the source of the neutrino prediction.
Origin
Higgs, Englert, Brout, Guralnik, Hagen and Kibble (1964); Koide (1983); Foot (1994); Brannen (2006).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Lepton masses as a squared relation matrix.

9. Quark masses

quark Yukawa terms
What it does
The Higgs couplings of the six quarks.
Role in the framework
The top, bottom, charm and strange masses are measured inputs. The Cabibbo relation gives the strange-to-down ratio (19.9 against 19.8 ± 0.3), and the η → 3π decay with Leutwyler's relation gives the up quark (2.04 MeV against 2.16 ± 0.07) without lattice input. The 45° relations among the heavier quarks are patterns good to 1–10%, not laws.
Origin
Yukawa (1935); Cabibbo (1963); Kobayashi and Maskawa (1973).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The far-side press on the down quarks.

Part II. Equations used for comparison with experiment

10. Relational distance

Sij = ½ ln(mi/mj), (mi + mj)/√(mimj) = 2 cosh Sij
What it does
Measures how far apart two masses are in a way that does not depend on units or on an overall scale.
Role in the framework
Applied to every pair of the 19 known particles, it gives the particle-to-particle matrix (171 pairs), which is extended with antiparticles, decays, quarks and gluons.
Origin
The logarithmic scale is standard in renormalisation; its use as a relational distance between particles is this framework's.

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Relational distance and the scale field.

11. Koide relation

Q = (me + mμ + mτ) / (√me + √mμ + √mτ)² = 2/3
What it does
A relation among the three charged-lepton masses, satisfied by the measured masses to about one part in 300,000.
Role in the framework
Equivalent to links of exactly 1/√2 in the lepton matrix, which is the statement that the shared part and the relational part of the mass vector are equal (each 941.6 MeV).
Origin
Koide (1983); geometric form, Foot (1994).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The lepton 45° balance (Koide's relation).

12. Neutrino masses

(m₁ + m₂ + m₃) / (−√m₁ + √m₂ + √m₃)² = 2/3, m₂² − m₁² = Δm²₂₁, m₃² − m₁² = Δm²₃₁
What it does
The same relation applied to neutrinos, with the two measured mass-squared splittings.
Role in the framework
Solved, it gives m₁ = 0.36, m₂ = 8.6 to 8.7 and m₃ = 50.1 to 50.3 meV (JUNO 2026 against the NuFIT 6.0 splittings). No solution exists for inverted ordering within the cosmological bound, which is why the prediction can fail.
Origin
Splittings from NuFIT 6.0 (2024); sign choice as in Brannen (2006).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The seesaw: why neutrinos are light.

13. Containment balance (bag model)

E(R) = (4π/3) B R³ + Σ xq/R − Z₀/R + Eπ, M = √(E² − ⟨P²⟩)
What it does
The energy of quarks confined in a sphere of radius R: the containment pressure costs energy that grows with volume, while quark motion costs energy that falls with size. The minimum fixes the radius and the mass.
Role in the framework
Fitting the proton and Δ masses alone gives B = 60.1 MeV/fm³ (57.1–63.7 for a ±5% pion coupling) and a quark-core rms radius of 0.725 fm.
Origin
Chodos, Jaffe, Johnson, Thorn and Weisskopf (1974); Théberge, Thomas and Miller (1980); Donoghue and Johnson (1980).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The bubble: size and energy.

14. Stellar structure (TOV)

dp/dr = −(ε + p)(m + 4πr³p) / [r(r − 2m)], dm/dr = 4πr²ε
What it does
The equations of hydrostatic balance for a star in general relativity: pressure gradient (push) against gravity (pull).
Role in the framework
Integrated with the quark-matter equation of state below, they give the maximum neutron-star mass and radius.
Origin
Tolman (1939); Oppenheimer and Volkoff (1939).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Stellar structure (Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff).

15. Quark-matter equation of state

p(μ) = (3/4π²)[a₄ μ⁴ − (ms² − 4Δ²) μ²] − B, a₄ = 1 − 2αs/π
What it does
The pressure of deconfined quark matter at chemical potential μ, including the strong interaction (a₄), the strange-quark mass and a pairing gap Δ.
Role in the framework
B is taken from the proton and αs from measurement, so the only unknown is the gap. A gap of about 82–97 MeV satisfies both the pulsar-mass and tidal limits.
Origin
Freedman and McLerran (1977); Fraga, Pisarski and Schaffner-Bielich (2001); Alford, Braby, Paris and Reddy (2005).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The bubble: size and energy.

16. Tidal deformability

Λ = (2/3) k₂ / C⁵
What it does
How strongly a neutron star is stretched by its companion in a merger; k₂ is the Love number and C = GM/(Rc²) the compactness.
Role in the framework
Compared with the GW170817 limit Λ1.4 ≲ 580.
Origin
Hinderer (2008); LIGO and Virgo (2018).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Stellar structure (Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff).

17. Compactness and Buchdahl's limit

C = GM/(Rc²), C < 4/9 for any static star
What it does
A single number measuring how contained an object is: about 0.000002 for the Sun, 0.18 for a neutron star, 4/9 at the limit of stability, 1/2 for a black hole.
Role in the framework
The containment scale for stars: below 4/9 an object is under-contained and can leak; at 1/2 it is fully contained.
Origin
Buchdahl (1959).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Stellar structure (Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff).

18. Friedmann equation

H(a)² = H₀² (Ωm a⁻³ + Ωr a⁻⁴ + ΩΛ), Ωm + Ωr + ΩΛ = 1, t₀ = ∫₀¹ da / (a H)
What it does
How the expansion rate depends on the contents of the universe, and the age that follows.
Role in the framework
The full-medium principle is the condition that the shares sum to one. With Planck 2018 values the age is 13.79 billion years.
Origin
Friedmann (1922); Lemaître (1927); Planck Collaboration (2020).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The Friedmann equation.

19. Resistance of space

n(r) = 1 + 2GM/(rc²), δ = 4GM/(bc²)
What it does
An effective refractive index around a mass, and the resulting deflection of light passing at distance b.
Role in the framework
The factor 2 requires the resistance to act on time and space equally. At the solar limb δ = 1.75″.
Origin
Eddington (1920); Gordon (1923); Shapiro (1964).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The outskirts of galaxies.

20. Entanglement area law and Jacobson's argument

Sent = η A, δQ = T dS ⇒ Gμν + Λgμν = (8πG/c⁴) Tμν, G = 1/(4η) (in units ħ = c = kB = 1)
What it does
In a filled medium, entanglement across a boundary grows with its area. Requiring the Clausius relation at every local horizon then yields Einstein's equations, with Newton's constant fixed by the entanglement density η.
Role in the framework
This is the route by which gravity follows from entanglement. A lattice of coupled oscillators confirms the area law (boundary fit 80 times better than volume).
Origin
Bombelli, Koul, Lee and Sorkin (1986); Srednicki (1993); Jacobson (1995).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Gravity from entanglement.

21. Entangled correlations

E(a, b) = −cos(a − b)
What it does
The correlation between spin measurements at angles a and b on an entangled pair. It violates Bell's inequality, so no local hidden-variable model reproduces it.
Role in the framework
Read as one structure observed from two places.
Origin
Bell (1964); Clauser and Freedman (1972); Aspect, Grangier and Roger (1982).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Bell's inequality (CHSH form).

22. Uncertainty

Δx · Δk ≥ ½
What it does
A property of every wave: the narrower it is in space, the broader its spread of wavelengths.
Role in the framework
Heisenberg's relation is this statement applied to matter waves; in the framework it expresses seeing one perspective of a single geometry at a time.
Origin
Heisenberg (1927).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

23. Anomaly cancellation

Σ Y = 0, Σ Y³ = 0, … ⇒ Qu = ½ + 1/(2N), Qd = −½ + 1/(2N)
What it does
Consistency conditions that a quantum gauge theory must satisfy. For one generation with N colours they fix all hypercharges.
Role in the framework
For N = 3 they give +2/3 and −1/3. Anomalies do not fix N; three colours follow from the octonionic inner space (see Results) and agree with measurement.
Origin
Geng and Marshak (1989); Minahan, Ramond and Warner (1990).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: One spot, one facing: the forces.

24. Crossing-wave geometry

z = cos x + cos y, ∬cell K dA = 0
What it does
The simplest surface made of crossing waves. Its Gaussian curvature K is positive at peaks and troughs and negative at saddles, and integrates to zero over each cell.
Role in the framework
The transitioning-geometry principle: always curved locally, flat on average.
Origin
Gauss–Bonnet theorem; Morse (1934).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The wave equation.

25. Black-hole ringdown

Mω = 1.5251 − 1.1568 (1 − χ)0.1292, Q = 0.7000 + 1.4187 (1 − χ)−0.4990
What it does
The frequency and damping of the dominant vibration of a spinning black hole of mass M and spin χ.
Role in the framework
For GW150914 this gives 250 Hz and 4.0 ms. A fully contained object has no reflecting surface, so no echoes are expected.
Origin
Kerr (1963); Berti, Cardoso and Will (2006); LIGO and Virgo (2016).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Einstein's field equations.

26. Lepton lifetimes

ττ = τμ · BR(τ → eνν̄) · e−10 S, S = ½ ln(mτ/mμ)
What it does
The tau lifetime from the muon lifetime. The factor e−10S is (mμ/mτ)⁵, the fifth-power dependence of weak decay rates on mass.
Role in the framework
Lepton universality written in relational form: 289.7 fs against 290.3 fs measured.
Origin
Fermi (1934).

27. Cabibbo relation

sin θC ≈ √(md/ms) = eSds
What it does
Relates the mixing of down and strange quarks to their mass ratio.
Role in the framework
0.2242 against 0.2243. In the quark-mass chain it is the step that gives the down-quark mass.
Origin
Gatto, Sartori and Tonin (1968).

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The Cabibbo relation and the down quark.

Measured inputs

Put together as one calculation, the framework takes about 28 measured numbers, against about 34 for the Standard Model with gravity and cosmology counted the same way (the 28 assumes every pending measurement agrees; counting only savings already confirmed it is 32, plus six stated choices; see Inputs and predictions): the three force strengths; the Higgs value; the lepton scale and twist; five quark masses (the down quark follows from the Cabibbo angle); four quark-mixing numbers and the strong-force angle; two neutrino splittings; the four neutrino mixing numbers that the mixing pattern does not fix; gravity's strength; the amount of dark energy; the amount of dark matter; the matter excess; and the two numbers for the seeds of galaxies. Saved and confirmed by measurement: the tau mass and the down quark mass. The lightest neutrino mass is saved only if pending measurements agree. Saved only if pending measurements agree: the Higgs mass and two neutrino mixing numbers. One force strength is saved as well if the strong coupling lands at 0.1178. The lepton saving comes through the 45° balance, which also fixes the neutrino masses; beyond the count, the framework says which forces and particles exist, which the Standard Model assumes.
