How to read any equation

An equation is a sentence. The equals sign is the verb: it says the left side and the right side are the same amount. Letters stand for quantities you can measure, and the operators say what to do with them. Read left to right, do what is inside brackets first, then powers and roots, then multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction.

1. Newton's second law

F = m a

Already existed Newton (1687).

Read it aloud

Force equals mass times acceleration.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
Fthe force: the push or pull on an object (newtons, N)
="is equal to": the two sides are the same amount
mthe mass: how much the object resists being speeded up or slowed down (kilograms, kg)
athe acceleration: how quickly the speed changes (metres per second, per second, m/s²)
m atwo letters side by side mean multiplication: m × a
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Measure the object's mass m.
  2. Measure how fast its speed changes, a.
  3. Multiply them: that is the force that must be acting.
  4. Example: a 2 kg ball speeding up by 3 m/s every second needs F = 2 × 3 = 6 N.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The rule connecting force to change of motion.

Why it is needed. Things keep moving steadily unless something acts; this equation says how much they change when something does.

How it works. A bigger force gives a bigger change; a bigger mass gives a smaller change for the same force.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. Used whenever the framework compares with everyday mechanics. It is the slow, weak-gravity limit of relativity, which the framework keeps.

What it replaces or changes. Nothing; it replaced Aristotle's idea that motion needs a constant cause.

Sources. Newton, I. Used on this site: All of physics: motion and force.

2. Newton's law of gravity

F = G m₁ m₂ / r²

Already existed Newton (1687); G first measured by Cavendish (1798).

Read it aloud

The pull between two masses equals G times the first mass times the second mass, divided by the distance squared.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
Fthe pull of gravity between the two (N)
GNewton's constant, 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m²/kg², the strength of gravity; measured, not derived
m₁, m₂the two masses (kg); the small numbers below the letters are labels, not powers
rthe distance between their centres (m)
r²r to the power 2, which means r × r
/"divided by"
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Multiply the two masses.
  2. Multiply by G.
  3. Divide by the distance times itself.
  4. Example: 1 kg at the Earth's surface: G × (5.972 × 10²⁴) × 1 ÷ (6.371 × 10⁶)² = 9.82 N, which is your weight per kilogram.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The rule for the strength of gravity in everyday conditions.

Why it is needed. It explained falling apples and orbiting planets with one law.

How it works. Twice the distance gives a quarter of the pull, because the pull spreads over a sphere whose area grows as r².

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. The framework keeps it as the weak-gravity limit of Einstein's equation. Gravity's strength G is one of the framework's measured inputs: every attempt to derive it failed.

What it replaces or changes. Replaced by general relativity where gravity is strong or speeds are high.

Sources. Newton, I; Cavendish, H; CODATA recommended values of the fundamental physical constants: 2022. Used on this site: All of physics: gravity; Problems: gravity.

3. Noether's theorem: why energy is conserved

∂μ jμ = 0

Already existed Noether (1918).

Read it aloud

The flow of a conserved quantity has no sources or sinks: what flows out of a region is exactly what the region loses.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
jμa current: the density and flow of a conserved quantity such as energy or charge
μan index running over time and the three directions of space (0, 1, 2, 3)
∂μthe rate of change along direction μ (a partial derivative)
repeated μa letter appearing once up and once down means add the four terms (Einstein's summation rule)
= 0the total is exactly zero: nothing is created or destroyed
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Choose a symmetry of the laws, for example "the laws are the same tomorrow".
  2. Noether's construction gives a current j for that symmetry.
  3. Its total change (time part plus flow through space) is zero.
  4. For time symmetry the conserved quantity is energy; for space symmetry, momentum; for rotation, angular momentum.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The link between symmetry and conservation.

Why it is needed. It explains why energy, momentum and charge are conserved instead of just observing it.

How it works. If the rules do not change when you shift time, the quantity that time-shifts generate stays constant.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. The framework's "the amount is kept" (rule 2) is local conservation in this sense, checked locally, since an expanding universe has no global total.

What it replaces or changes. Nothing; it unified separate conservation laws under one principle.

Sources. Noether, E. Used on this site: All of physics: energy.

4. The wave equation

∂²u/∂t² = v² ∇²u

Already existed d'Alembert (1747).

Read it aloud

How fast the displacement's rate of change changes in time equals the wave speed squared times how curved the displacement is in space.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
uthe displacement: how far the medium is pushed from rest at each place and time
ttime
∂²/∂t²the second rate of change in time: the acceleration of the displacement
vthe speed of the wave
∇²"del squared": the total curvature of u in space (adds the second rates of change along x, y and z)
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Where the shape is curved, the medium is pulled back toward flat.
  2. That pull accelerates the displacement.
  3. The result is a pattern that moves at speed v.
  4. Example: u = A cos(kx − ωt) solves it when ω = v k.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The equation for waves in any springy medium: sound, water, light, gravitational waves.

Why it is needed. It describes energy moving through a medium while the medium stays in place.

How it works. Curvature gives a restoring push; push gives acceleration; acceleration passes the shape along.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. The framework reads waves as energy spreading through regions of different resistance of the medium, and its geometry as crossing waves that average to flat.

What it replaces or changes. Nothing.

Sources. d'Alembert, J. Used on this site: All of physics: waves.

5. Maxwell: the speed of light from electricity and magnetism

c = 1 / √(μ₀ ε₀)

Already existed Maxwell (1865).

Read it aloud

The speed of light is one divided by the square root of the magnetic constant times the electric constant.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
cthe speed of light, 299,792,458 m/s
μ₀the magnetic constant, about 1.2566 × 10⁻⁶ in SI units: how strongly currents make magnetism
ε₀the electric constant, 8.8542 × 10⁻¹² in SI units: how strongly charges make electric fields
√the square root: the number that, multiplied by itself, gives what is inside
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Multiply the two constants: 1.2566 × 10⁻⁶ × 8.8542 × 10⁻¹² = 1.1127 × 10⁻¹⁷.
  2. Take the square root: 3.336 × 10⁻⁹.
  3. Divide 1 by it: 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s, the speed of light.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. A prediction from Maxwell's four equations that electromagnetic waves travel at this speed.

Why it is needed. It revealed that light is an electromagnetic wave.

How it works. A changing electric field makes a magnetic one and vice versa; the pair carries itself forward at speed c.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. Kept unchanged. Its symmetry (Lorentz, not Galilean) leads to special relativity, which the framework keeps.

What it replaces or changes. Replaced the idea of light as a separate substance.

Sources. Maxwell, J. Used on this site: All of physics: light.

6. Boltzmann's entropy

S = kB ln W

Already existed Boltzmann (1877).

Read it aloud

Entropy equals Boltzmann's constant times the natural logarithm of the number of arrangements.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
Sentropy: how spread out or disordered the energy is
kBBoltzmann's constant, 1.381 × 10⁻²³ J/K
lnthe natural logarithm: the power you must raise e ≈ 2.718 to, to get the number
Wthe number of microscopic arrangements that look the same from outside
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Count the ways W the parts can be arranged while looking the same overall.
  2. Take its logarithm.
  3. Multiply by kB.
  4. Example: 100 coins all heads: W = 1, S = 0. Half heads: W ≈ 1.0 × 10²⁹, S ≈ 66.8 kB. That is why shaken coins end up mixed.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The bridge between the microscopic world and heat.

Why it is needed. It explains the second law: systems move toward states with more arrangements.

How it works. Logarithms turn multiplied counts into added entropies, so the entropy of two independent parts adds.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. Kept. Black-hole entropy and the area law for entanglement, which the framework uses for gravity, are its descendants.

What it replaces or changes. Replaced the idea of heat as a fluid (caloric).

Sources. Boltzmann, L; Bekenstein, J. Used on this site: All of physics: heat; Problems: time's direction.

7. Special relativity: energy, mass and the slowing of clocks

E² = (pc)² + (mc²)², so E = mc² at rest; γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²)

Already existed Einstein (1905).

Read it aloud

Energy squared equals momentum times light speed, squared, plus mass times light speed squared, squared. For something at rest, energy equals mass times the speed of light squared.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
Eenergy (joules, J)
mmass (kg)
cthe speed of light
pmomentum, mass times velocity at low speed
γgamma, the factor by which moving clocks slow and moving lengths shrink
vthe speed of the moving object
+add
−subtract
Step by step, with numbers
  1. For a body at rest p = 0, so E = mc².
  2. Example: 1 gram: E = 0.001 × (3 × 10⁸)² = 9 × 10¹³ J, about the energy of 21 kilotons of TNT.
  3. For v = 0.6c: v²/c² = 0.36; 1 − 0.36 = 0.64; √0.64 = 0.8; γ = 1/0.8 = 1.25, so moving clocks run 25% slow.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The rules of space and time for steady motion.

Why it is needed. Light's speed is the same for everyone, so time and length must adjust.

How it works. Mass is energy held in place; moving energy adds momentum.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. Kept. In the framework, mass is the energy held at the balance point of a bubble; E = mc² is the exchange rate between held and moving energy.

What it replaces or changes. Replaced absolute time and Newton's unlimited speeds.

Sources. Einstein, A. Used on this site: All of physics: special relativity; All of physics: bubbles.

8. Einstein's field equations

Gμν + Λ gμν = (8πG/c⁴) Tμν

Already existed Einstein (1915); Hilbert (1915). Edited by this framework derived from entanglement (Jacobson, 1995), read as the medium's resistance.

Read it aloud

The curvature of spacetime, plus the cosmological constant times the metric, equals eight pi G over c to the fourth times the energy and momentum present.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
gμνthe metric: the rule for measuring distances and times at every point
Gμνthe Einstein tensor: how spacetime is curved, built from the metric
Λlambda, the cosmological constant (dark energy)
Tμνthe stress-energy: how much energy, momentum and pressure are present
8πG/c⁴the conversion factor between energy and curvature; π ≈ 3.1416
μ, νlabels for the four directions of spacetime; there are ten independent equations
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Write down where energy and pressure are (T).
  2. Solve for the metric g that makes the left side match.
  3. Free bodies then follow the straightest paths in that metric.
  4. Example: light passing the Sun's edge bends by 4GM/(c²R) = 8.49 × 10⁻⁶ radians = 1.75 arcseconds, as measured in 1919.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The law of gravity as geometry.

Why it is needed. Gravity's pull on light and the slowing of clocks need curved spacetime.

How it works. Energy tells spacetime how to curve; spacetime tells matter how to move.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. The framework does not add this term by hand: it follows from entanglement (equation J below). It reads curvature as the medium's resistance acting equally on time and space. The factor of two in light bending is inherited, not independently derived.

What it replaces or changes. Replaced Newton's instantaneous gravity.

Sources. Einstein, A; Hilbert, D; Jacobson, T. Used on this site: Equations explained: gravity; Mathematics: light in a gravitational field; All of physics: general relativity.

9. Light comes in packets

E = h ν, λ = h / p

Already existed Planck (1900); Einstein (1905); de Broglie (1924).

Read it aloud

A packet of light's energy equals Planck's constant times its frequency. Any particle's wavelength equals Planck's constant divided by its momentum.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
hPlanck's constant, 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s: the size of one quantum of action
νnu, the frequency: waves per second (hertz)
λlambda, the wavelength
pmomentum
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Green light has ν ≈ 5.6 × 10¹⁴ Hz.
  2. E = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 5.6 × 10¹⁴ = 3.7 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, about 2.3 electronvolts per photon.
  3. That is why green light can trigger the chemistry of vision but radio waves cannot: each packet is too small.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The founding rule of quantum physics.

Why it is needed. It explained the colours of hot objects and the photoelectric effect.

How it works. Energy is exchanged in whole packets; matter also has a wave side.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. Kept. The framework reads particle and wave as two perspectives on one geometry.

What it replaces or changes. Replaced the idea that light energy can be divided without limit.

Sources. Planck, M; Einstein, A; de Broglie, L. Used on this site: All of physics: the quantum.

i times h-bar times the rate of change of the wavefunction equals the energy operator acting on the wavefunction.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
ψpsi, the wavefunction: the list of possibilities and their amplitudes

What it is. The equation of motion for quantum states.

11. Born's rule: probabilities from amplitudes

P(a) = |⟨a|ψ⟩|²

Already existed Born (1926).

Read it aloud

The probability of getting result a is the square of the size of the overlap between the state and a.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
P(a)the probability of result a
⟨a|ψ⟩the amplitude: how much of the state ψ lies along the outcome a (a complex number)
| |absolute value bars: the size of a number, ignoring its direction
²squared
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Write the state as a sum over outcomes: ψ = 0.6|a⟩ + 0.8|b⟩.
  2. Square the sizes: 0.36 and 0.64.
  3. They add to 1: a 36% chance of a, 64% of b.
What it is, why it is, how it works

What it is. The link between the mathematics and what is measured.

Why it is needed. Measurements give single results with predictable frequencies.

How it works. Amplitudes add like waves; probabilities are their squared sizes, which produces interference.

Where it fits in the framework, and what it replaces

Where it fits. Kept as an inherited rule. The framework reads a measurement as taking one perspective; it does not derive the rule.

What it replaces or changes. Nothing.

Sources. Born, M. Used on this site: Problems: measurement.

12. Heisenberg's uncertainty principle

Δx · Δp ≥ ħ/2

Already existed Heisenberg (1927); exact form Kennard (1927).

Read it aloud

The spread in position times the spread in momentum is at least h-bar over two.

Every symbol and operator
SymbolWhat it means
Δxdelta x, the spread (uncertainty) in position
Δpthe spread in momentum
·multiplication
≥"is greater than or equal to"
ħh-bar
Step by step, with numbers
  1. Confine an electron to Δx = 10⁻¹⁰ m (an atom's size).
  2. Then Δp ≥ 1.055 × 10⁻³⁴ / (2 × 10⁻¹⁰) = 5.3 × 10⁻²⁵ kg m/s.
  3. Divide by the electron mass: a speed spread of at least 580 km/s. That jitter is why atoms do not collapse.
What it is, why it is, how it works

31. How the force strengths change with energy

Already existed Gell-Mann and Low (1954); Gross and Wilczek, Politzer (1973); Georgi, Quinn and Weinberg (1974); two loops Machacek and Vaughn (1983–85). Added by this framework the framework's far-side weak pairs and heavy D change the bi above their masses.

Read it aloud

SymbolWhat it means

Step by step, with numbers

Only the part of curvature that is not a uniform stretch is tied to the part of energy that is not a uniform background.
