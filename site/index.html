A Theory of Everything, by Matthew Gelfman and Melissa Behrends-Gelfman

Dynamic Relational Transitioning Geometry

Matthew Gelfman and Melissa Behrends-Gelfman

A relational, entanglement-based picture of the universe. Particles are there one by one, but what matters is how they relate, and those relationships form the matrices the theory is built from. It reproduces known physics from a few plain principles, and it makes predictions that experiments can check.

TRPG does not try to replace anyone. In Matthew's words, the goal is to show that "all the great physicists were correct but seeing the same thing in different perspectives."

Abstract

Dynamic Relational Transitioning Geometry (TRPG) describes the universe through the relationships between its constituents rather than through the constituents taken one at a time. It rests on five rules and one bet. (1) Everything is one matrix of relationships, seen one perspective at a time, with entanglement first: matter is held states on its diagonal, forces are the links between them, the Higgs is the link across one state, and space and time are the geometry of the relationships. (2) Every bound structure is a pull balanced by a resistance (containment), and mass is that balance. (3) Within the lepton families a particle’s tie to itself balances its ties to the others. (4) Nothing extra. (5) The universe started smooth; whether a short stretching followed is left to measurement. The bet is that the far side of the relationship matrix is real. Earlier versions stated rules 1–4 as three ideas and one rule. The whole framework fits on one page. It is also told as a story anyone can read, and set beside the other theories of everything, each with its own story.

Combined with established physics, these principles reproduce the deflection of starlight by the Sun (1.75″), the age of a flat universe (13.79 billion years), a containment pressure of B = 60 (57–64) MeV/fm³ from the proton and Δ masses alone, neutron stars inside the observed window of 2.0–2.15 solar masses, consistent with gravitational-wave data, once quarks in their cores pair, and the three charged-lepton masses from a single relational matrix with one scale. The strong force, with exactly three colours, follows from the same principles once the medium's inner space is required to have no gaps, and the relationship matrix seen from one perspective gives, with stated readings, which other forces exist and, read as a relationship among three, every particle of a generation with its measured charge, with matter one-handed. Where physics has already established a result, from the W and Z masses to atomic binding and the tau lifetime, the framework reaches it by a second route, and each such check is listed with who established it and how well the two agree. The force strengths meet if three far-side weak pairs of the same matrix sit at 2.4 × 108 GeV, which requires a strong coupling of 0.1178; this awaits measurement and is not counted as established until it returns. The heavy quark masses and quark mixing, the lepton twist, the matter–antimatter excess, the upper limit of three generations and dark matter's mass and amount are not derived and remain open (the main line's dark matter candidate is a chargeless wave, the majoron); gravity's strength belongs with the force strengths as one problem, and why the Higgs side settles is open too. The forces that bind, the strong and the electric, are the largest part of the forces that cannot tell which way the observer faces, and from them the proton is colour-neutral and the atom electrically neutral, exactly. The one mass the Higgs does not give predicts that neutrinos are their own antiparticles.

Four pictures behind the theory

The theory, explained in layers

Relationships matter most

Each particle is real on its own, but the universe is how particles relate. Every particle compared with every other gives the first matrix. The proton sits at the scale of everything we physically see, so it governs our view.

Space is a full bag

Space is like liquid filling a sealed bag. Nothing sits between the liquid and what is in it, because the liquid is space. A glass that is half water and half air is still a full glass.

Containment decides

Outward push against inward pull decides whether something is a particle, a star or a black hole. A star is under-contained and a black hole is over-contained.

Entanglement comes first

Gravity is a byproduct of entanglement. Uncertainty means we see one side of a single geometry at a time. Entangled partners stay joined through the geometry.

The predictions that could prove it wrong

Neutrinos follow the same relational rule as the electron, muon and tau. With today's limit from cosmology on their total, that rule gives normal ordering, with masses of 0.36, 8.6–8.7 and 50.1–50.3 meV, which add up to 0.0591 to 0.0594 eV, depending on the measured splittings.

The JUNO experiment and sky surveys such as DESI can test this within a few years. If neutrinos turn out to have inverted ordering, or a clearly different total, this part of TRPG is wrong. The total is only just above the smallest total normal ordering allows (0.0588 eV), so the most distinctive number is the lightest mass, 0.36 meV.

Second, with B set by the proton, neutron stars pass both tests only if quarks in their cores pair up with a pairing gap of about 82 to 97 MeV (75 to 108 across the proton's range of B). Theory estimates 50 to 150 MeV, and it has not been measured yet.

Third, if neutrino flavours are perspectives of one neutrino (the TM1 mixing pattern), the matter–antimatter phase of neutrino mixing should be near 270° (253–292°). Current data lean towards 180–215° but cannot yet decide; DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande will.

Fourth, the strengths of the forces meet only if the strong coupling is 0.1178. The most precise measurement so far, 0.11873 ± 0.00056, lies 1.7 standard deviations above it and does not yet decide; lattice calculations at about 0.3% will. All ten dated predictions are listed on the Results page.

Where it stands, honestly

Against the nine requirements a Theory of Everything must meet, TRPG scores 7 PASS and 2 PARTIAL, with every script rerun. It takes about 28 measured numbers, against about 34 for the Standard Model with gravity and cosmology counted the same way (the 28 assumes every pending measurement agrees; counting only savings already confirmed it is 32, plus six stated choices; see Inputs and predictions). The tau mass and the down quark mass are saved and confirmed by measurement; the lightest neutrino mass, the Higgs mass (127.2 ± 1.5 GeV from a rule at the grain of space, against 125.13 ± 0.11 measured) and two neutrino mixing numbers are saved only if pending measurements agree. No known measurement contradicts it.

Both partial items were attacked as hard as we could, and both stay PARTIAL. Force groups (awaiting measurement): the strong force, with exactly 3 colours, follows from the full-bag rules through the octonions. The main picture reads the other forces from the 3 × 3 relationship matrix, seen from one perspective and read as a relationship among three. It gives the measured 8, 3 and 1 force carriers (a published count, reproduced here), a whole generation with the measured charges, the up-quark and electron partners included, and one-handed matter in the measured arrangement, each with a stated reading. Only the strengths remain. With the Standard Model's particles alone they miss by 8.9%. They meet if three colourless far-side weak pairs of the same matrix sit at 2.4 × 108 GeV, and that requires a strong coupling of 0.1178. The most precise measurement so far is 0.11873 ± 0.00056, which does not yet decide, so this is not counted as established until the measurement returns. “At most 3” generations rests on the nothing-extra rule and on measurement. Quark masses (awaiting a rule): two links pass strict tests: the down quark from the Cabibbo angle, and the up quark at 2.04 MeV from η → 3π with no lattice input (within 0.4σ with the decay value of Leutwyler's Q, whose 3% uncertainty makes this a loose test; 2.0σ with the lattice value). The top, bottom, charm and strange masses and the quark mixing are measured inputs. The strongest lead, one heavy far-side state pressing on the down quarks, fits all ten quark numbers, but with nine free numbers, so it is a lead and not a result. The 45° relations among the heavy quarks are patterns good to 1–10%, not laws; charm misses by 15–19 standard deviations.

The heavy neutrino partners that the lepton matrix implies do not predict the matter–antimatter excess: with the lepton rule read literally they fall about 4,000 times short of the observed 6.1 × 10⁻¹⁰. The observed value is allowed through a cold start that hands its energy straight to those partners, at the cost of one measured start number. That is allowed, not predicted. Dark matter's amount is one measured number. It is read as partly perspective; the matrix allows one candidate for the rest, a cold chargeless wave (the majoron), but gives no number for it, so it is a lead and not part of the core. Neither partial item has been solved by anyone in physics, and neither is forced here.
