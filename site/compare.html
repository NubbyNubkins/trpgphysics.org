How to use this page

Each theory below has its own story, written so anyone can follow it. Read the stories first if you like, then come back here to compare them.

This page is meant to stay useful whether or not TRPG turns out to be right. Every theory is described as fairly as we can manage, with the people who built it named. Where TRPG is weaker, we say so.

Four words used below:

  • Measured numbers: values a theory cannot work out and must be told, like the mass of the electron. Fewer is simpler.
  • Prediction: a number a theory says before it is measured. One prediction made beforehand is worth more than many matches found afterwards.
  • Peer review: other experts check the work before it is published. It catches mistakes. It does not prove a theory right.
  • Falsified: shown wrong by experiment. A good theory says clearly what would falsify it.

What changes under peer review

The established theories have already been reviewed for years, so review changes them little. TRPG has not, so here is what a careful reviewer would most likely say about it.

What a reviewer would likely accept:

  • TRPG’s statement is clear and can fail: three ideas, one rule (“nothing extra”) and one bet (a hidden far side is real).
  • It keeps a long, public list of its own failed ideas.
  • Counting measured numbers the same way for every theory is fair.
  • Its neutrino predictions are clean tests: the lightest mass (0.36 meV), a sum of 0.0591 to 0.0594 eV, and normal ordering.

What a reviewer would likely challenge:

  • The grain of space. It was set at the Planck energy divided by √2, chosen from several options when the strong coupling was already known. The Planck energy alone would give 0.1196 instead of 0.1178. Until a new measurement arrives, the 0.1178 counts as a fit, not a prediction.
  • The tau mass comes from Koide’s formula (1982). For leptons the 45° balance follows from the family symmetry and maximum uncertainty between self and relationship; it does not hold for quarks.
  • The Higgs rule is borrowed from Shaposhnikov and Wetterich. It fits overall (1.4 standard deviations) but needs a top mass 3.7 standard deviations below the measured one.
  • Neutron stars. The quark pairing gap of 82 to 97 MeV is needed to fit the observed heaviest stars. A reviewer would call it chosen, not predicted.
  • The forces and charges come from a stated reading of the octonion table, on top of other people’s work.

If TRPG turns out to be false

The Standard Model, Einstein’s gravity, the octonion mathematics, the seesaw and Koide’s formula do not depend on TRPG. They would all still stand. What would be lost is TRPG’s particular way of joining them: the view that the universe is a table of relationships seen one perspective at a time. Even then, the tests it proposed would have told us something true about nature.
