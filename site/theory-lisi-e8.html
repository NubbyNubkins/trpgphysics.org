Theories of everything

An Exceptionally Simple Theory: the Story of Lisi’s E8

The surfer-physicist who tried to fit every particle into one beautiful shape, and what happened when the mathematicians checked.

Chapter 1. The most beautiful shape

Among all the symmetry shapes mathematicians know, one stands out. It is called E8. It has 248 directions of symmetry. Drawn on paper, it looks like a stunning flower made of 240 points. It was first found by Wilhelm Killing and Élie Cartan in the 1880s and 1890s. In 2007 a team of mathematicians took four years and a supercomputer just to map its structure completely.

Chapter 2. A bold idea from Maui

Garrett Lisi earned a PhD in physics and then lived on the island of Maui, surfing and thinking about physics independently. In 2007 he posted a paper called “An Exceptionally Simple Theory of Everything.”

His idea was that every particle and every force, gravity included, is one of the 248 directions of E8. Each particle sits at a point of the flower. The forces are rotations that move one point to another. In one shape, everything.

The paper went viral. Newspapers around the world covered the surfer who might have found the theory of everything.

Chapter 3. The check

In 2010 two mathematicians, Jacques Distler and Skip Garibaldi, did the careful check. They proved that E8 cannot contain the particles of our world in the way Lisi needed. In particular, it cannot hold three generations of matter with the correct “handedness” (the fact that the weak force acts only on left-spinning particles) together with gravity. Their paper was titled, plainly, “There is no Theory of Everything inside E8.”

Chapter 4. Afterwards

Lisi kept working and has proposed modified versions, but the original theory, as stated, does not work. Its predicted new particles, about twenty of them, have not been seen.

The story is worth telling anyway. A beautiful idea was put forward openly, checked openly, and found wanting. That is how science is meant to work, and it says nothing bad about the person who tried.

The theory at a glance

Main idea Every particle and force is part of the one symmetry shape E8
New ingredients about 20 new particles
Got right drew wide public interest in the deep mathematics of symmetry
Failed E8 cannot hold three generations with the right handedness plus gravity (Distler and Garibaldi, 2010)

How it differs from TRPG

  • The shared hope. Both hope one structure holds everything. TRPG also uses an “exceptional” structure, the octonions and the E6 family of 27, which is a smaller cousin of E8.
  • What TRPG learned. One mathematical shape is not enough by itself. TRPG gets handedness from the side you observe from and the generations as three placements of one shape, and it checks every claim against measurement. Lisi’s story shows why a public list of a theory’s own failures matters, and TRPG keeps one.

The people who built it

Garrett Lisi; Killing and Cartan (E8); Distler and Garibaldi (the check); the Atlas of Lie Groups team (the 2007 map of E8).
