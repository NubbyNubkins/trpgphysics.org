2. The action

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The master equation (the action).

The framework is written as two actions: a fundamental one, valid from the grain of space down, and an effective one for the inside of hadrons and compact stars. Every term has mass dimension four. The curvature term is not put in by hand: it emerges from entanglement (Section 14).

S = ∫ d⁴x √(−g) [ ½ M̄P² R − ¼ Σ F² + Σ ψ̄ iγμDμ ψ + |DH|² − V(H) − ( (√2/vH) L̄L Sℓ² eR H + quark terms + far-side terms + h.c. ) − ½ (MN N̄cN + h.c.) ]
(3)
Seff = ∫ d⁴x √(−g) [ q̄ iγμ∂μ q + ½(∂P)² − (λ/4)(P² − v²)² − gP P q̄q ]
(4)

P is an effective field of the strong force, in the manner of Friedberg and Lee (1977): it describes the gluon vacuum inside hadrons and compact stars and is not added to the fundamental action, so the strong force is not counted twice. It has mass dimension one, so gP is a pure number. Seff is used only as a classical, mean-field description of the bag, not as a weakly coupled quantum field theory.

V(P) = (λ/4)(P² − v²)²
(5)

3. Field equations

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Einstein's field equations.

The entanglement route (Jacobson, 1995) yields only the trace-free part of Einstein’s equations, with the cosmological constant appearing as a constant of integration; the metric is therefore varied at fixed volume element (unimodular gravity). Varying S at fixed volume element, and Seff with respect to P and q̄:

Gμν − ¼ R gμν = 8πG (Tμν − ¼ T gμν) (the constant part of V(H) does not curve space)
(6)
□P = −V′(P) − gP q̄q = −λ P (P² − v²) − gP q̄q
(7)
no scale field: the relational scale S = ½ ln(m₁/m₂) is a coordinate on mass ratios, not a field
(8)
(iγμ∂μ − gP P) q = 0
(9)

4. Containment potential

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The containment field (the bubble wall).

B ≡ V(0) − V(v) = λ v⁴ / 4
(10)
mG² = 2 λ v² (curvature at the outside vacuum, identified with the lightest glueball)
(11)
⇒ λ = mG⁴ / (16 B), v = mG / √(2λ), wall thickness ≈ ħc / mG
(12)

With B = 60.1 MeV/fm³ = 4.62 × 10⁸ MeV⁴ and mG = 1710–1730 MeV:

λ = 1157–1212, v = 35.1–35.5 MeV, wall ≈ 0.115 fm
(13)

5. Relational distance

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: Relational distance and the scale field.

Require a distance d(mi, mj) between masses that is invariant under m → km, antisymmetric, and additive along a chain.

d(k mi, k mj) = d(mi, mj) ⇒ d = f(mi/mj)
(14)
f(xy) = f(x) + f(y) ⇒ f(x) = c ln x
(15)
Sij ≡ ½ ln(mi/mj) (c = ½, so that eS = √(mi/mj))
(16)
Mij ≡ (mi + mj)/√(mimj) = eSij + e−Sij = 2 cosh Sij
(17)

A scale field σ, which coupled to nothing, is dropped from earlier drafts: Sij is a coordinate on mass ratios, not a field.

6. Charged-lepton masses

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The lepton 45° balance (Koide's relation); Three needles: the lepton masses from one scale.

Sℓ = M₀ · circ(1, r eiδ, r e−iδ)
(18)

A circulant matrix is diagonalised by the discrete Fourier transform; its eigenvalues are

√mk = M₀ [ 1 + 2r cos(δ + 2πk/3) ], k = 0, 1, 2
(19)

Sum and sum of squares:

Σ √mk = 3M₀, Σ mk = 3M₀² (1 + 2r²)
(20)
Q ≡ Σ mk / (Σ √mk)² = (1 + 2r²)/3
(21)
r = 1/√2 ⇔ Q = 2/3 ⇔ 3M₀² (shared part) = 3M₀² · 2r² (relational part)
(22)

Measured masses give

Q = 0.6666645, 3M₀² = 941.55 MeV, δ = arccos[ (√mτ/M₀ − 1)/√2 ] = 0.222239 ± 0.000040
(23)

Setting δ = 2/9 and fixing M₀ from mτ:

me = 0.51098 MeV (−0.003%), mμ = 105.6561 MeV (−0.002%)
(24)

7. Neutrino masses

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The seesaw: why neutrinos are light.

(m₁ + m₂ + m₃) = ⅔ (s₁√m₁ + s₂√m₂ + s₃√m₃)², si = ±1
(25)
m₂ = √(m₁² + Δm²₂₁), m₃ = √(m₁² + Δm²₃₁) (normal ordering)
(26)

With (s₁, s₂, s₃) = (+,+,+) there is no solution. With (−,+,+):

m₁ = 0.36 meV, m₂ = 8.66 meV, m₃ = 50.13 meV, Σm = 0.0592 eV (NuFIT 6.0 splittings; with JUNO 2026: m₂ = 8.60 meV, Σm = 0.0591 eV)
(27)

Inverted ordering (m₃ lightest) has a solution only with Σm ≥ 0.10 eV, excluded by DESI (Σm < 0.064 eV, 95%).

8. Quark-mass relations, tested strictly

Explained symbol by symbol and step by step: The Cabibbo relation and the down quark; The far-side press on the down quarks.

Each relation contained in the particle matrices is tested with measured masses on both sides and judged against the measurement's own error bar. Inputs: the measured bottom, charm and strange masses.

yt = 1 ⇒ mt = vH/√2 = 174.1 GeV (measured 172.60 ± 0.27; +0.9%, +5.6σ: fails strictly)
(28)
Q(c, b, t) = 2/3 ⇒ mc = 1353 MeV (measured 1272.9 ± 4.5; +14.8σ: fails)
(29)
Q(s, c, b) = 2/3 ⇒ ms = 84.5 MeV (measured 92.9 ± 0.7; −10σ: fails)
(30)
sin θC = √(md/ms) ⇒ ms/md = 19.9 (measured 19.8 ± 0.3; +0.3σ: passes)
(31)
mu/md = (m²K⁺ − m²K⁰ + 2m²π⁰ − m²π⁺) / (m²K⁰ − m²K⁺ + m²π⁺) = 0.559 (Weinberg 1977, Dashen's theorem)
(32)

This leading-order ratio needs an electromagnetic correction. The η → 3π decay avoids it (Sutherland 1966; Bell and Sutherland 1968):

(mu/md)² + (ms/md)²/Q² = 1, ms/md = 19.87, Q = 22.1 ± 0.7 (Leutwyler 1996; Colangelo, Lanz, Leutwyler and Passemar 2018)
(33)
⇒ mu/md = 0.437 ⇒ mu = 2.04 ± 0.28 MeV (range 1.76–2.32; measured 2.16 ± 0.07; inside the range; no lattice input)
(34)

The 45° relations among the heavier quarks are numerical patterns good to 1–10% (Rivero 2011), not laws. Earlier, a chain starting from mb fed its own high charm value (1384 MeV) into the strange step and so looked better than it is.

Scale-independent balance angles, all masses run to a common scale at two loops:

θ = arccos √(1/(3Q)): θup = 52.15°, θdown = 48.06°, θleptons = 45.00°
(35)

ΣN = −(3f²/4π²mπ²) [ IN + (32/25) IΔ ], I = ∫ k⁴ u²(kR) / [ω(ω + ΔE)] dk, u(x) = 3j₁(x)/x, f²/4π = 0.0778
